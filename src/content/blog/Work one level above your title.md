No matter where I am or what my title is, I want my work to be a step ahead of it.

## One level up

If I'm a junior developer, I work to a mid-level developer's standard. When I reach mid-level, I work toward senior. And so on.

When I earn a promotion, I don't settle at the effort that got me there. Once the work I'm doing starts to match my new role, that's my signal to raise the bar again.

## Leading from where you are

I want to be a leader at work, whatever my title says. That means spearheading projects and making sure my team is moving in the same direction, with the same goals.

That's why I don't get comfortable. I hold myself to a higher standard than my role requires.

This kind of effort gets noticed when you work with leadership that values growth. Keep growing in what you know and what you deliver, and work to be better than you were yesterday. People notice, and you learn more along the way.

## Why it's possible now

This used to be really hard to pull off. A single person could only do so much.

That's changed. What AI can do now, and how quickly these tools are becoming more capable and easier to use, is immense. In the last four months I've delivered more than I ever thought one person could in that time.

The sky is the limit. Make what you want. Make what you need. Make what you can.

As your ideas grow, so does your potential. That's the mindset that keeps you working one level above your title.
